Parent Summary: Core Rules/Concepts Summary: In D&D, damage type immunity means you don't take damage of a specific type, while immunity to a condition means it doesn't affect you. Healing restores hit points up to your maximum; excess healing is lost. Dropping to 0 hit points results in death or unconsciousness unless stabilized. Instant death can occur through monster death, a hit point maximum of 0, or massive damage exceeding the character's maximum. Characters at 0 hit points must make Death Saving Throws, with rolling a 1 resulting in two failures and a 20 restoring 1 hit point. Damage while at 0 hit points causes failure(s) in these throws, potentially leading to death if it equals or exceeds the maximum. A stable condition can be achieved through a successful Wisdom (Medicine) check, preventing further Death Saving Throws until healed.

Parent Summary: Core Rules/Concepts Summary: In D&D, characters can move double their Speed on their turn if they Dash, and may use special speeds like Fly or Swim instead of regular movement. A dead creature cannot regain Hit Points without magical revival, which requires the spirit's consent. The Deafened condition prevents hearing and causes automatic failure in ability checks requiring it. Characters with 0 Hit Points must make a Death Saving Throw to avoid death. Creatures need specific amounts of water daily based on size; insufficient intake leads to Exhaustion from dehydration. Difficult Terrain doubles movement cost, affecting spaces with obstacles like creatures, furniture, or steep slopes.
Content:
A player character must make a Death Saving
Throw (also called a Death Save) if they start their
