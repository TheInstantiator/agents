A single failed conversion—due to a fumble, penetration, or holding penalty—hands the ball to the opponent at their own 1-yard line on downs. While that pins them deep, it's no guarantee of dominance: a savvy offense can kneel for a safety (losing just 2 points) or execute a quick screen pass for a long gain, flipping field position and igniting their momentum. Suddenly, you're not asserting dominance; you're praying they don't break free for a touchdown of their own, as seen in infamous flops like the 2018 Saints' failed fourth-down try from the 1 against the Rams, which cost them a Super Bowl shot.

Contrast this with the field goal: a chip shot from the 18-yard line (after snapping from the 1) boasts a 99% success rate league-wide, per ESPN stats, delivering 3 guaranteed points without risking turnover. In close games—where 70% of NFL contests are decided by a touchdown or less, according to Football Outsiders—those 3 points can be the margin of victory, turning a tie into a lead or extending one to ice the clock. Advanced win-probability models from sources like nflfastR confirm this: in neutral game states (mid-fourth quarter, tied score), kicking yields a 5-10% higher win probability than going for it, especially against stout defenses. Going for the touchdown isn't bold heroism; it's hubris that invites chaos, as evidenced by coaches like Bill Belichick regretting aggressive calls in losses (e.g., 2009 Vikings game). The field goal embodies disciplined strategy: secure the points, control the game, and let the opponent chase. In football's high-stakes theater, victory favors the prudent, not the presumptuous—oppose the motion and champion the field goal!
