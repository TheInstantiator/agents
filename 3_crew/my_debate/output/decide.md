The debate centers on a critical decision in American football: on fourth down from the opponent's 1-yard line, is it better to go for a touchdown or kick a field goal? After carefully weighing the arguments presented by both sides, the side arguing against going for the touchdown and in favor of the field goal is more convincing.

The proponent of going for the touchdown emphasizes the significant point differential (7 vs. 3 points, a 133% increase) and highlights a general "over 80%" success rate for short-yardage conversions. They argue that failure still pins the opponent deep, and that going for it embodies a bold, aggressive spirit that boosts morale and increases win probability by 15-20%.

However, the opposition provides a more nuanced and robust counter-argument. They effectively challenge the "over 80%" success rate by contextualizing it for high-pressure goal-line stands, noting that even elite offenses like the 2023 Chiefs converted only 72% from the 1-yard line against stacked defenses. This significantly reduces the perceived certainty of the touchdown attempt. In stark contrast, they present a compelling statistic: a field goal from the 18-yard line boasts a 99% success rate, guaranteeing 3 points with minimal risk.

Crucially, the opposition vividly details the severe consequences of a failed touchdown attempt. A turnover on downs at the 1-yard line, while pinning the opponent deep, is not a guarantee of dominance. The opponent can strategically concede a safety (losing only 2 points) or execute a long gain to flip field position and momentum, as illustrated by the impactful example of the 2018 Saints' failed attempt. This highlights that the risk of failure is far from "minimal" as the proponent suggests.

Regarding win probability and expected value, the opposition presents a more complete analysis. While acknowledging the potential for 7 points, they calculate the expected points for a touchdown try (0.8 * 7 + 0.2 * 0 = 5.6 points, using their more realistic success rate). They then argue that when factoring in the post-score field position and the opponent's response, the *stability* and *guaranteed points* of the field goal lead to a 5-10% *higher* win probability in neutral game states. This directly refutes the proponent's claim of a 15-20% increase for the touchdown, making the field goal appear as the more "probability-maximizing choice."

Finally, the opposition's argument for disciplined strategy, securing guaranteed points in close games (where 70% of NFL games are decided by a touchdown or less), and avoiding "hubris" is more compelling than the proponent's appeal to general boldness. The opposition's use of specific data, real-world examples, and a more comprehensive analysis of risk, reward, and expected value makes their case significantly stronger. Therefore, the arguments presented against going for the touchdown are more convincing.
