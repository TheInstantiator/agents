The motion debated is: It is better to go for the touchdown on the opponent's 1-yard line on fourth down than to go for the field goal in American football.

After carefully weighing the arguments presented by both sides, the side arguing that it is better to go for the field goal is more convincing.

However, the opposition for the field goal effectively counters these points by presenting a more comprehensive and realistic assessment of the risks and consequences. They highlight the "30% failure rate" for a touchdown attempt, even from the 1-yard line, and contrast it with the "over 99% conversion" rate for a field goal from that range. This stark difference in certainty for guaranteed points is a powerful argument.

They argue that failing a touchdown attempt from the 1-yard line does not merely pin the opponent; it "gifts them prime field position to launch a game-changing drive, often flipping momentum catastrophically." This is substantiated with specific historical examples (2011 Saints, 2022 Eagles), which lend significant weight to their argument. Furthermore, they contend that a botched touchdown attempt is far more demoralizing to a team and emboldening to the opponent than a routine field goal.

While the allure of 7 points and the "fortune favors the bold" philosophy are compelling, the field goal side's emphasis on "cold calculus," "risk management," and "sustainable success" (citing Belichick's Patriots) provides a more grounded and convincing strategic perspective. The guaranteed 3 points, without the high peril of a turnover on downs and the potential for a catastrophic momentum swing, is presented as the more prudent and ultimately more effective choice in tight contests.

The certainty of points, coupled with the severe potential downsides of a failed gamble, makes the field goal the more strategically sound option based on the arguments presented.
